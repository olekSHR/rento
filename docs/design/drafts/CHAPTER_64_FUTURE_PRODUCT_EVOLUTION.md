## 1. Purpose

This chapter defines the foundation authority for **Future Product Evolution** as a Product Design Standard macro-domain.

Future Product Evolution exists because Rento must be able to evaluate future product capabilities without converting future ideas into approved scope, implementation commitments, feature roadmaps, or engineering architecture. Approved Chapters 1–63 govern the current Product Design Standard authority. Future Product Evolution provides the principles-level discipline for deciding how future capabilities may relate to that authority.

The central subject of this chapter is:

**How future product capabilities are evaluated, inherited, bounded, and authorized before they receive Product Design Standard authority.**

This chapter does not approve any future feature. It does not create specialized chapters. It does not start Architectural Audit. It does not authorize Project Architecture & Standards, Product Development Methodology, implementation, delivery planning, release planning, APIs, data models, ownership teams, or technical architecture.

---

## 2. Architectural Position

Future Product Evolution follows Performance Experience completion and remains inside Phase 1 - Product Design Standard.

It exists before Product Design Standard v1.0 completion because the standard governs present and future design decisions. Without a future evolution foundation, Rento would have approved authority for current product experience but no durable product-design discipline for evaluating future capabilities before they become part of the standard.

Future Product Evolution is:

- a Product Design Standard macro-domain;
- a principles-level authority for future capability evaluation;
- an extension discipline for approved architecture;
- a guard against feature-roadmap drift;
- a boundary between product authority and implementation ambition.

Future Product Evolution is not:

- a roadmap;
- a backlog;
- an implementation plan;
- a technical architecture;
- a delivery methodology;
- a version release plan;
- an AI, chat, maps, real-time, or automation feature catalog;
- an Architectural Audit substitute.

Future Product Evolution consumes Chapters 1–63 without redefining them.

Approved authority remains owned by the chapters and macro-domains that created it:

| Approved authority | Future Product Evolution relationship |
|--------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Chapters 1-12 Foundation | Consumes product philosophy, principles, structure, visual language, interaction, component, and form authority |
| Search Experience | Consumes search and discovery authority without expanding maps, ranking, saved search, or search continuity into future capability scope |
| Decision Experience | Consumes judgment, media, comparison, viewing, application, and commitment authority without changing decision progression |
| Housing Obligation | Consumes legal, financial, and occupancy readiness authority without creating execution systems |
| Settled Tenancy | Consumes tenancy lifecycle authority without becoming property management |
| Realtor Platform | Consumes realtor professional participation authority without operating realtor business |
| Admin Platform | Consumes delegated governance execution authority without expanding admin power |
| Design System Governance | Consumes standard governance boundaries without governing the standard lifecycle itself |
| Accessibility & Internationalization | Consumes inclusive and multilingual product meaning authority without redefining accessibility, localization, or translation |
| Performance Experience | Consumes future feature boundary and performance integrity without redefining perceived performance |

Future Product Evolution may identify when a future capability requires new Product Design Standard authority. It may not take ownership away from any approved chapter.

---

## 4. Extension, Not Replacement

Future product evolution must extend approved architecture. It must never silently replace it.

Extension means:

- preserving the meaning of approved concepts;
- inheriting existing boundaries before proposing new authority;
- using approved terminology where it already exists;
- adding new product authority only when a documented gap exists;
- keeping future capability evaluation traceable to current authority.

Replacement means:

- changing the meaning of an approved concept to fit a future capability;
- treating a future feature as more authoritative than the standard;
- bypassing approved chapter boundaries;
- duplicating an existing chapter's authority under a new name;
- using implementation pressure as a reason to change product architecture.

Replacement is not allowed.

---

## 5. Future Capability Evaluation

A future capability is any product possibility that has not yet received Product Design Standard authority.

Future capability evaluation determines whether such a possibility:

1. is already governed by Chapters 1–63;
2. requires only interpretation of existing authority;
3. requires new Product Design Standard authority;
4. belongs outside Product Design Standard scope;
5. needs Design Council authorization before authoring;
6. should be deferred because its product meaning is not yet architecturally clear.

Future capability evaluation must happen before a capability receives:

- product authority;
- ownership;
- execution depth;
- inclusion in a future product version;
- specialized Product Design Standard coverage.

Examples may include AI assistance, AI communication, maps, live updates, chat, push synchronization, real-time collaboration, or future interaction models. These examples are evaluation subjects only. They are not approved scope, not planned features, not committed roadmap items, and not implementation requirements.

---

## 6. Evaluation Questions

Every future capability must be tested against these questions before it receives Product Design Standard authority:

1. What user, role, journey, state, or trust problem does the capability claim to address?
2. Which approved chapters already govern that problem?
3. Does existing authority fully answer the product question?
4. If existing authority is insufficient, what exact architectural gap remains?
5. Is the gap product-design authority, engineering architecture, delivery methodology, or roadmap planning?
6. Would authorizing the capability redefine an approved concept?
7. Would the capability transfer ownership from an existing chapter?
8. Would the capability create misleading user confidence, status, availability, verification, performance, or obligation meaning?
9. Does the capability require new terminology, or can it inherit existing terminology?
10. Is the capability mature enough for Product Design Standard authority, or should it remain deferred?

If these questions cannot be answered, the capability is not ready for Product Design Standard authority.

---

## 7. Authority Inheritance

Authority inheritance is the rule that future capabilities must consume approved Product Design Standard authority before requesting new authority.

Authority inheritance requires:

- Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 product philosophy preservation;
- trust, moderation, verification, and source-of-truth preservation where relevant;
- state and communication continuity where relevant;
- Accessibility & Internationalization meaning preservation;
- Performance Experience integrity and future feature boundary preservation;
- role, journey, and lifecycle separation;
- platform posture preservation.

Future capabilities may combine multiple approved authorities, but combination does not create ownership transfer. A future capability that touches search, trust, performance, and accessibility must preserve all four authorities rather than absorbing them into a new feature-owned model.

---

## 8. Future Feature Boundary

Future capabilities do not automatically expand the authority of existing macro-domains.

A future capability may interact with Performance Experience, Accessibility & Internationalization, Design System Governance, Search Experience, Realtor Platform, Admin Platform, or other approved authorities. Interaction is not ownership.

For example:

- a real-time capability may involve Performance Experience, but it does not redefine Performance Experience;
- an AI communication capability may involve communication and trust, but it does not become approved merely because communication already exists;
- a map-related capability may involve location experience, but it does not automatically expand map authority;
- a chat capability may involve contact and inquiry stewardship, but it does not bypass existing contact boundaries.

Each future capability requires independent architectural evaluation.

---

## 9. Product Evolution Principles

### 9.1 Future Capability Legibility

Rento must be able to distinguish a future capability idea from an approved product authority. A possibility is not a standard.

### 9.2 Architectural Readiness Before Product Depth

No future capability should receive deep product treatment before its relationship to approved authority is clear.

### 9.3 Extension Over Novelty

Novelty is not architectural justification. A future capability is justified only when it extends the standard in a way existing authority cannot already cover.

### 9.4 Trust Before Expansion

Future product expansion must preserve trust, source of truth, availability honesty, role clarity, moderation clarity, and obligation meaning.

### 9.5 Boundary Clarity Before Capability Ownership

A future capability cannot own product meaning until its boundaries with existing chapters are clear.

### 9.6 Deferral Is Valid

If a future capability is not architecturally clear, deferral is the correct outcome. Product authority should not be granted merely to keep pace with market trends, implementation interest, or roadmap pressure.

### 9.7 No Feature Catalog

Future Product Evolution may define evaluation discipline. It must not become a catalog of possible features.

### 9.8 No Implementation Shortcut

Implementation feasibility does not create Product Design Standard authority. Engineering feasibility belongs to later phases.

---

## 10. Version Continuity Principles

Future Product Evolution may define how future product versions preserve architectural continuity. It must not invent future version numbers, release plans, milestones, or schedules.

Version continuity means:

- future versions inherit approved authority unless explicitly amended through governance;
- future capability additions must be traceable to Product Design Standard authority;
- future changes must preserve concept lineage;
- future scope growth must not make current approved chapters ambiguous;
- future evolution must remain reviewable by Design Council governance.

Version continuity does not mean:

- defining v1.1, v2.0, or any release plan;
- sequencing future features;
- committing to implementation milestones;
- defining engineering migration paths;
- declaring compatibility guarantees outside product-design authority.

## 12. Boundaries

### 12.1 Design System Governance Boundary

Design System Governance governs the Product Design Standard lifecycle. Future Product Evolution governs product capability evolution. These must not be merged.

### 12.2 Standards Evolution Boundary

Chapter 58 governs controlled evolution of the standard itself. Future Product Evolution governs how future product capabilities are evaluated before receiving product authority. A capability may create pressure to evolve the standard, but that pressure does not itself authorize standard evolution.

### 12.3 Accessibility & Internationalization Boundary

Accessibility & Internationalization governs inclusive and multilingual access to product meaning. Future Product Evolution may evaluate future capabilities that involve language, translation, or accessibility, but it does not redefine Chapter 62.

### 12.4 Performance Experience Boundary

Performance Experience governs perceived performance as trust-preserving product experience. Future Product Evolution preserves Chapter 63 Future Feature Boundary and does not redefine performance authority.

### 12.5 Project Architecture & Standards Boundary

Project Architecture & Standards is a future phase after Product Design Standard v1.0 approval. This chapter does not define frameworks, APIs, data models, security standards, infrastructure, implementation governance, or engineering standards.

### 12.6 Product Development Methodology Boundary

Product Development Methodology is a future phase after Project Architecture & Standards. This chapter does not define delivery processes, rituals, workflows, operating models, ownership teams, release ceremonies, or methodology.

### 12.7 Architectural Audit Boundary

Product Design Standard v1.0 Architectural Audit is Phase 2. It starts only after Phase 1 is complete and all Product Design Standard chapters are authored and individually approved. This chapter does not begin audit.

## 13. Future Capability Evaluation States

Future capabilities may occupy one of these product-design evaluation states:

| State | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Unexamined Capability** | A possibility has been named, but no Product Design Standard evaluation has occurred |
| **Inherited Capability** | Existing Chapters 1–63 already provide sufficient product authority |
| **Gap Candidate** | Existing authority may be insufficient, but the gap requires Design Council review |
| **Deferred Capability** | Product meaning is not clear enough for Product Design Standard authority |
| **Authorized Product Authority Candidate** | Design Council has authorized Product Design Standard authoring for a documented gap |
| **Out-of-Scope Capability** | The subject belongs to implementation, delivery, engineering standards, methodology, or roadmap planning |

These states are product-design governance states only. They are not workflow states, ticket statuses, backlog statuses, release states, or implementation stages.

## 2. Design Council Decision

**Status:** APPROVED WITH CLARIFICATIONS

The independent Design Council review approves Performance Experience as a single future foundation authority, subject to the clarifications integrated below.

The approved architectural conclusion remains:

- Performance Experience is required because Rento lacks one cross-cutting product authority for perceived performance as trust-preserving experience.
- The remaining gap is exclusively product-level authority.
- One future foundation chapter remains sufficient.
- Execution chapters remain unjustified.
- No chapter number is assigned during Phase 1.
- No chapter authoring begins through this integration.

## 3. Foundation-Only Architecture

Performance Experience requires exactly one future foundation authority.

This macro-domain must not introduce execution chapters, additional architectural layers, or specialized dimension registries. Existing Chapters 1-62 already provide local performance-related coverage across journeys, roles, states, communication, trust, accessibility, and governance. Performance Experience exists only to establish the missing cross-cutting product authority for perceived responsiveness, trust under latency, waiting experience, continuity under delay, interruption recovery, degraded experience, and preservation of user confidence.

The future foundation authority must consume existing local coverage rather than replace it.

---

## 4. Performance Integrity

Performance Experience must define a canonical product principle preventing false perception of completion.

The future foundation chapter must explicitly state:

**The product must never create a misleading perception that an operation has completed merely to appear faster. Perceived responsiveness must never compromise product truth.**

This principle belongs to product architecture only. It must remain implementation-independent and must not introduce technical guidance, latency targets, optimization methods, instrumentation requirements, release gates, or engineering metrics.

Performance Integrity protects the distinction between honest responsiveness and deceptive speed theater. A product may acknowledge user action quickly, preserve context during waiting, and communicate progress clearly, but it must never imply completion, success, availability, approval, delivery, synchronization, publication, moderation outcome, verification outcome, or recovery before the product truth supports that meaning.

---

## 5. Future Feature Boundary

Future capabilities do not automatically expand Performance Experience authority.

This applies to future capabilities including, but not limited to:

- AI assistance
- AI communication
- maps
- live updates
- chat
- push synchronization
- real-time collaboration
- future interaction models

Every future capability must undergo independent architectural evaluation before it receives product authority, specialized ownership, or execution depth.

Performance Experience governs only product-level responsiveness and trust preservation during latency, waiting, interruption, degraded experience, delayed state, or slow operation. It does not become the architectural owner of future feature behavior merely because those features include responsiveness concerns.

---

## 6. Product vs Engineering Boundary

Performance Experience explicitly excludes:

- frontend optimization
- backend optimization
- infrastructure
- networking
- database performance
- caching
- CDN
- rendering optimization
- bundle optimization
- API latency targets
- observability
- profiling
- benchmarking
- load testing
- engineering metrics
- SLO/SLA
- CI/CD
- implementation guidance
- delivery methodology

These remain responsibilities of future Project Architecture & Standards or Product Development Methodology.

Product requirements may later inform engineering standards, but Performance Experience does not define how technical systems achieve speed, reliability, measurement, monitoring, deployment quality, or delivery process.

---

## 7. Authority Consumption

Performance Experience consumes but never redefines:

- Trust
- State Architecture
- Communication
- Accessibility & Internationalization
- Search Experience
- Housing Journey
- Realtor Platform
- Admin Platform

No ownership transfer is permitted.

Chapter 20 remains authoritative for trust, verification, moderation, marketplace integrity, appeals, fraud, transparency, and trust recovery meaning. Chapter 24 remains authoritative for empty, loading, waiting, error, recovery, state continuity, and state integrity. Chapter 25 remains authoritative for feedback, status, progress, and system communication. Chapter 62 remains authoritative for inclusive and multilingual access to product meaning.

Search Experience, Housing Journey, Realtor Platform, and Admin Platform retain their local journey and role responsibilities. Performance Experience may define cross-cutting responsiveness and confidence principles only where those principles preserve product-level trust without taking ownership of source-domain meaning.

---

## 8. Architectural Economy

Architectural economy remains mandatory:

- one foundation chapter remains sufficient;
- execution chapters remain unjustified;
- no chapter numbering occurs during Phase 1;
- no authoring begins through this artifact;
- no additional architectural layers are introduced;
- no approved Chapters 1-62 are modified.

A future execution chapter would require a documented material architectural gap and explicit Design Council approval. Future chapter count, future feature complexity, or symmetry with prior macro-domains is not sufficient justification.
